# Neon Requiem — Backgrounds (Initial Playtest Roster)

**Status:** Approved for initial testing; open to changes after playtest  
**Recorded:** 21 Nov 2026  
**Coverage:** Twelve backgrounds replacing the inherited D&D 5e background list  
**Build status:** Design only. These have not been implemented or tested in Foundry.

## Choosing a background

A background describes the life a character led before the campaign. **Class is what you can do now; background is where you came from.** A former corporate employee might become a Vanguard, just as a freight-hauler might become a Cipher. Backgrounds do not determine which class or subclass a player may take.

Each character chooses **one** background at character creation. Every background provides:

- **Two skill proficiencies.** These use familiar 5e skill names for initial testing; the Neon Requiem skill list is still being reviewed.
- **One tool proficiency.** Tool names in this draft are working labels, not a finalized item/tool taxonomy.
- **One Background Edge.** A modest, situational advantage drawn from experience, contacts, or familiarity with a place or institution.
- **One Connection and one Complication.** The listed prompts are examples. The player and GM should turn them into specific people, obligations, and problems that suit the campaign.

If a background proficiency duplicates a class or racial proficiency, choose another appropriate proficiency **of the same category**. The final choices must be settled in the general proficiency rules.

**Every Human and Synth may choose any background.** Backgrounds never add starting Cyberware, installed Hacks, Augmentation slots, ability-score increases, feats or class levels. Humans still start with one installed Cyberware item; Synths start with two; both have six body-owned augmentation slots.

### Running Background Edges

The GM supplies information the character could plausibly recognize or uncover using the stated method. An Edge is **not** unrestricted permission, guaranteed classified knowledge, a free item, or an automatic success on a dangerous task. When an outcome is contested or uncertain, use a suitable normal check and adjudicate the Edge's fictional advantage without inventing a permanent numeric bonus.

A Connection is a person or network with a reason to know the character, **not** a guaranteed source of free equipment or favors. A Complication provides a possible plot hook, not an automatic penalty applied every session.

## The twelve backgrounds

### 1. Blockborn

*You know which streets belong to whom, which stairwells are watched, and which neighbors will actually open their doors.*

- **Skills:** Insight, Sleight of Hand
- **Tool:** Street repair kit
- **Background Edge:** **Neighborhood Web.** After an hour of local inquiries in a familiar kind of neighborhood, identify a useful local contact, current dispute, or practical shortcut.
- **Connection prompt:** A neighborhood fixer, tenant organizer, shop owner or trusted neighbor still knows you.
- **Complication prompt:** Someone from the old block still expects you to settle a debt.

### 2. Corporate Exile

*You used to belong to the system. Then you were fired, framed, discarded, or walked away.*

- **Skills:** Deception, Investigation
- **Tool:** Forgery kit
- **Background Edge:** **Corporate Procedure.** After reviewing an organization's public documents or observing its office operations, identify the department likely responsible for a particular decision or process. This doesn't grant access or credentials.
- **Connection prompt:** A former colleague, administrative contact or union representative might still take your call.
- **Complication prompt:** Your old employer still has a file on you.

### 3. Convoy Runner

*You grew up on freight routes, border crossings, supply depots, and roads that disappeared from official maps years ago.*

- **Skills:** Survival, Perception
- **Tool:** Ground vehicle handling
- **Background Edge:** **Road Ledger.** Given a map and available travel information, identify a plausible resupply stop, alternate route, or likely road hazard before setting out.
- **Connection prompt:** A driver, dispatcher or depot worker remembers your old route.
- **Complication prompt:** A former convoy crew believes you abandoned them.

### 4. Contract Laborer

*You built the city, maintained its machines, or worked off a contract designed to keep you employed forever.*

- **Skills:** Athletics, Investigation
- **Tool:** Mechanic's tools
- **Background Edge:** **Shop-Floor Eye.** After inspecting an accessible industrial workspace, identify its routine workflow, an ordinary mechanical hazard, or a plausible manual shutdown point.
- **Connection prompt:** A former shift supervisor, maintenance worker or shop steward recognizes you.
- **Complication prompt:** The contract may have ended, but its obligations didn't.

### 5. Security Washout

*You wore the uniform. You know how security teams think, and you know exactly how much protection a badge really offers.*

- **Skills:** Perception, Intimidation
- **Tool:** Security equipment kit
- **Background Edge:** **Patrol Grammar.** After ten minutes observing a routine security operation, identify its apparent patrol pattern, shift procedure, or checkpoint habits. The prediction can become outdated if security changes its response.
- **Connection prompt:** A former patrol partner, dispatcher or security trainer knows your history.
- **Complication prompt:** An old partner recognizes you—and remembers why you left.

### 6. Clinic Hand

*You stocked supplies, cleaned blood off the floor, watched surgeons work, and learned which emergencies could not wait.*

- **Skills:** Medicine, Insight
- **Tool:** Medical kit
- **Background Edge:** **Triage Habit.** After examining a patient with appropriate access, recognize the urgency of visible injuries and the kind of medical equipment or specialist the case calls for. This doesn't provide treatment by itself.
- **Connection prompt:** A nurse, clinic administrator or volunteer organizer can vouch for your time there.
- **Complication prompt:** A former patient remembers a decision you made during a crisis.

### 7. Signal Pirate

*You ran unauthorized broadcasts, listened to frequencies nobody admitted using, or kept local communications alive when the official grid went dark.*

- **Skills:** Investigation, Performance
- **Tool:** Radio and signal equipment
- **Background Edge:** **Airwave Ear.** With a functioning receiver and time to listen, identify the general source, type, or broadcast schedule of an accessible unencrypted signal. Encrypted networks still require proper access and Hacks.
- **Connection prompt:** An old listener, broadcast partner or equipment supplier recognizes your handle.
- **Complication prompt:** Someone is looking for the operator behind your old signal.

### 8. Syndicate Courier

*You moved packages, collected payments, and learned that asking what was inside was rarely good for your health.*

- **Skills:** Deception, Stealth
- **Tool:** Forgery kit
- **Background Edge:** **Dead-Drop Tradecraft.** With preparation and an accessible location, arrange a discreet physical message or package drop for a willing contact. It provides ordinary concealment, not immunity from surveillance or investigation.
- **Connection prompt:** A runner, dispatcher or fence knows where to leave a message for you.
- **Complication prompt:** A delivery you once made has resurfaced.

### 9. Street Journalist

*You chased stories that powerful people wanted buried. Some of your sources trusted you. Others regretted it.*

- **Skills:** Investigation, Persuasion
- **Tool:** Recording and media equipment
- **Background Edge:** **Open Record.** Given time to research public records and interview accessible sources, identify a credible lead, documented connection, or inconsistency worth investigating.
- **Connection prompt:** A source, editor, records clerk or independent publisher might still share a lead.
- **Complication prompt:** Publishing one story endangered someone who trusted you.

### 10. Scrap Diver

*Broken drones, abandoned machinery, crashed transports—someone else's trash kept you alive.*

- **Skills:** Survival, Investigation
- **Tool:** Salvage tools
- **Background Edge:** **Salvage Sense.** After examining accessible wreckage, identify likely useful component types, obvious recovery hazards, and whether the site is worth stripping. It doesn't generate free parts.
- **Connection prompt:** A yard foreman, parts trader or fellow salvager will sometimes hear you out.
- **Complication prompt:** You once recovered something its previous owner wants back.

### 11. Lab Subject

*You survived an experimental program, a clinical trial, or an institution that treated people as research material.*

- **Skills:** Medicine, Insight
- **Tool:** Laboratory equipment
- **Background Edge:** **Protocol Memory.** When examining laboratory procedures or medical testing equipment similar to those you experienced, recognize familiar methods and identify a plausible risk or investigative lead.
- **Connection prompt:** A former technician, fellow subject or whistleblower can confirm part of your history.
- **Complication prompt:** Your records contain something you were never supposed to learn.

### 12. Arcology Resident

*You lived behind managed doors, controlled climates, polished lobbies, and systems that kept the outside world at a distance.*

- **Skills:** History, Persuasion
- **Tool:** Administrative records kit
- **Background Edge:** **Service Etiquette.** You understand how managed facilities handle appointments, visitor requests, and minor administrative disputes. Given time and a plausible reason, you can find the correct public-facing service contact, though restricted access still requires authorization.
- **Connection prompt:** A concierge, neighbor or facilities employee remembers you from inside.
- **Complication prompt:** Someone inside the arcology believes you still owe them loyalty.

## Starting gear and money

Every background should eventually have a small starting-equipment package that fits the character's history: an ordinary tool, a worn badge, a personal receiver, work clothes, a notebook, or a similar memento. **Exact items, prices, starting money, and package values are not yet set.** Do not grant high-value Cyberware or scarce equipment through a background by default.

## Initial playtest checks

The first pass should ask whether:

1. Each background produces at least one useful noncombat moment without creating an automatic solution.
2. No Edge replaces a class signature ability (especially Cipher Hacks, Engineer fabrication, Lifeline treatment or Ripperdoc cybernetic surgery).
3. Contact benefits stay comparable across criminal, corporate, neighborhood and institutional backgrounds.
4. The proposed skills and tools can be mapped to real Foundry 5e proficiency fields without assuming that missing technical tool categories already exist.
5. Human and Synth characters can both use every background without conflicting with their racial starting Cyberware or augmentation slot ownership.
6. The Connection and Complication can be used in a campaign without forcing a player into a predetermined backstory or punishing their choice.
7. Starting equipment can be made equivalent in value once the gear and currency rules exist.

## Deferred design

This roster is **approved for initial testing**, not frozen as final rules. The following remain open: tool taxonomy, any revised skill list, replacement proficiency rules, Edge adjudication details, starting gear and funds, connections/complications on character sheets, and observed balance across actual play.

No game-system code, class progression, Cyberware system or release is authorized by this background-design record.
