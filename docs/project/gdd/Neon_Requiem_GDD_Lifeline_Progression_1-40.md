# Lifeline — Base Class Progression (Levels 1–40)

**Status:** Approved design; numerical balance pending  
**Recorded:** 21 Nov 2026  
**Subclasses:** See [Lifeline subclass features](Neon_Requiem_GDD_Lifeline_Subclasses_1-40.md)  
**Build status:** Design only; no game-system implementation yet.

## 1. Class chassis

**Lifeline** is the dedicated medical, healing, trauma-response, survival-support class. It translates the *roles* of 5e Cleric's healing, Bard's support and practical medical intervention into **technology and professional training**, without spells, divine magic or resurrection.

| Property | Accepted design |
| --- | --- |
| Hit Die | d8 |
| Primary ability | WIS |
| Secondary ability | INT |
| Saving throw proficiencies | WIS, CHA |
| Armor training | Light and medium |
| Weapon training | Simple weapons, compact firearms |
| Tool focus | Medicine, trauma care and medical technology (formal tool taxonomy TBD) |
| Signature resource | Treatment Dice: limited-use medical intervention dice |

**Subclasses** (conceptually approved, feature tables pending):
- **Trauma Tech:** Emergency care, surgery, stabilization and restoring combat readiness.
- **Nanomedic:** Nanotechnology, regenerative support and gradual recovery.
- **Combat Responder:** Mobile field medicine, extraction, protective interventions.

**Universal distinction:** Lifeline heals/treats patients; Engineer builds/repairs physical devices. A separately approved hybrid class, **Ripperdoc**, combines these competencies through level prerequisites. This does not erase either base class's identity.

## 4. Approved conceptual features

### Levels 1–10

- **Medical Training (1):** Medicine proficiency and medical tool proficiency; Expertise in Medicine; identify common injuries and needed specialist care.
- **Field Stabilization (1):** As an **action**, use an appropriate medical kit to stabilize an eligible creature at 0 HP without a Medicine check while within reach.
- **Treatment Dice (2):** Pool of **four d6**, used to power specialized medical interventions. Restored on a Short or Long Rest **provided the character can replenish/prepare the needed medical supplies**; exact supply rules pending.
- **Combat Treatment (2):** As an action, use a Treatment Die and suitable medical equipment on an eligible target within 5 feet, initially restoring HP equal to the rolled die + WIS modifier. Exact eligible-target/medical taxonomy TBD.
- **Rapid Treatment (5):** **Once per Short Rest**, use Combat Treatment as a Bonus Action, expending the normal Treatment Die.
- **Field Diagnostics (6):** Use appropriate equipment to identify injuries, conditions and proper treatment options.
- **Condition Care (9):** As an action, expend one Treatment Die and attempt to alleviate an eligible medical condition (example: Poisoned) through suitable treatment and an appropriate check; not universal automatic condition removal.
- **Preventive Measures (10):** During a Short Rest, prepare treatments for allies; each eligible recipient gains temporary HP equal to a spent Treatment Die roll + WIS modifier. Each recipient may benefit only once per Short Rest; one die per treatment.

### Levels 11–20

- **Improved Treatment (13):** Treatment pool increases to **6d8**. Spend up to **two** dice on one Combat Treatment, applying WIS modifier once.
- **Enhanced Recovery (13):** Improve Hit Dice recovery when treating an ally during a Short Rest; final scaling TBD.
- **Crisis Response (14):** **Once per Short Rest**, Reaction when an ally within 5 ft drops to 0 HP; if physically able to reach/treat them, immediately attempt Field Stabilization. Does not restore HP unless another feature/resource is expended.
- **Rapid Treatment II (17):** Two uses of Rapid Treatment per Short Rest.
- **Trauma Shield (18):** **Once per Short Rest**, spend an additional Treatment Die when treating an ally to grant temporary HP equal to its roll; does not stack with itself.

### Levels 21–30

- **Advanced Treatment (21):** Treatment pool increases to **8d10**; spend up to **three dice** per Combat Treatment.
- **Triage Link (22):** Through compatible connected medical equipment, monitor nearby/connected allies' vitals and treatment status within equipment communication range; does **not** itself grant remote healing.
- **Emergency Resuscitation (25):** **Once per Long Rest**, attempt an advanced intervention on an eligible patient recently reduced to 0 HP and still recoverable; on success restore HP via defined Treatment Dice use. **No resurrection of a character already dead.**
- **Condition Care II (26):** Expand the conditions that can be treated with suitable equipment and improve intervention checks.
- **Protective Infusion (29):** **Once per Short Rest**, administer preventive treatment granting temporary HP and a limited defensive benefit against one appropriate physiological/physical hazard; specifics TBD.
- **Master Treatment (30):** Treatment pool increases to **10d12**; spend up to **four dice** on one Combat Treatment.

### Levels 31–40

- **Critical Intervention (33):** **Once per Long Rest**, when treating an eligible creature that would otherwise remain at 0 HP, maximize the result of **one** spent Treatment Die.
- **Triage Link II (34):** Remotely activate a suitable medical device worn/carried/installed by a **consenting** ally if the device supports remote activation and a valid connection exists; all device costs/capacity/compatibility still apply.
- **Mass Casualty Protocol (37):** **Once per Long Rest**, with appropriate delivery equipment, administer emergency treatments to up to **three nearby eligible allies** in one action, spending **one Treatment Die per target**.
- **Last-Ditch Medicine (38):** **Once per Long Rest**, if Treatment Dice are empty and an eligible nearby ally drops to 0 HP, recover **one die** and use it for an immediate Combat Treatment as a Reaction.
- **Last Lifeline (40):** **Once per Long Rest**, activate a medical response state for **up to one minute**, with appropriate equipment/supplies and eligible targets:
  - Once per turn, use Rapid Treatment without spending one of its per-rest uses.
  - Once per turn, one Treatment Die expended on Combat Treatment is not deducted from the pool.
  - An eligible ally within **10 ft** who receives your medical treatment also gains temporary HP equal to your WIS modifier.
  - Field Stabilization may be used as a Bonus Action.
  - Does **not** provide unconditional resurrection, device-free healing, or infinite medical supplies.

## 6. Compatibility and hybrid boundary

- **Human and Synth** characters may require different medical procedures, devices and repair/biological treatments. Their exact patient/repair compatibility and interactions with Cyberware are not yet finalized.
- A Lifeline's healing is not a universal Engineer-like repair of machinery or an exception to Cyberware hardware requirements.
- **Ripperdoc** (separately approved special hybrid) requires **Engineer 5 + Lifeline 5** and has its own **20-level** hybrid progression; it performs integrated surgery and cybernetic hardware work. Its levels do not automatically advance Lifeline's subclass progression.
- Ripperdoc procedures are not a source of extra body-owned Augmentation slots or an exception to hardware-owned slot provenance.
- The broader 5e rules for HP, Hit Dice, conditions, death saving throws, and rests remain the design base, subject to individually approved Neon Requiem modifications.

## 7. Engineering / balance guardrails

The accepted concepts must be reconciled with: universal level 21–40 proficiency bonus progression, ability-score cap, feat cadence, multiclass HP/proficiency rules, healing throughput, death saves, condition taxonomies, supply/rest economy, interaction with player/NPC Cyberware, and server-authoritative Foundry automation.
